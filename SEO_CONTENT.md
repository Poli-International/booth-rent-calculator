## What is Booth Rent vs Commission Calculator?

The Booth Rent vs Commission Calculator is a free browser-based tool that works out the arithmetic of the two standard studio arrangements on the same revenue: a fixed weekly booth rent, or a percentage commission paid to the studio. You enter the deal parameters, and the tool shows the result from both sides of the table, for the artist and for the studio owner, then finds the monthly revenue at which the two cost the same.

The single figure the decision turns on is **Break-Even Monthly Revenue**. Below it, commission costs the artist less; above it, booth rent does. The tool also supports more complex real-world deals: tiered or sliding-scale commission across up to three monthly revenue brackets (with a blended effective rate), hybrid rent-plus-commission, a minimum weekly guarantee where the studio takes its commission or the floor, whichever is higher, and a walk-in versus custom client split that separates shop-provided foot traffic from appointments the artist brings in.

Beyond the headline comparison, the calculator models up to three competing studio offers side by side, each with its own rent, commission and deductions. It runs a realistic simulation that derives conservative, realistic and peak months from your working days, appointments per day, average ticket and no-show rate. It projects a 52-week annual cash flow with seasonal demand and time off. It gives the owner's view through a studio floor plan that models the station roster, fixed commercial overhead and occupancy. It assigns consumables and supplies, including laundry, sterilisation, card fees and utilities, to either the artist or the studio under each model. And it plots a break-even curve with four lines (artist and owner, each under booth rent and commission), each with its own pattern so it still reads when printed in black and white.

The tool shows numbers, not advice. It does not say which arrangement is better, and it says nothing about employment status, tenancy or tax, which differ by country. Everything runs in your browser; nothing you enter is sent anywhere.

## How to Use the Calculator

**Step 1: Enter the deal comparison inputs.** Start with booth rent per week, the studio's commission percentage, and working weeks per year. The comparison then shows, side by side, what the artist keeps and what the studio receives under each model.

**Step 2: Choose a commission structure.** Pick flat commission, or switch on **Tiered / Sliding-Scale Commission** to apply a different studio cut to up to three monthly revenue brackets and see the blended effective rate. You can also use **Hybrid Rent-Plus-Commission** to combine a lower base rent with a reduced commission cut, or **Minimum Guarantee** to give the studio its commission or a minimum weekly floor, whichever is higher.

**Step 3: Split walk-in and custom clients.** Use the **Walk-In vs Custom Client Split** to separate shop-provided foot traffic from appointments the artist brings in, so each revenue stream is costed under the correct model.

**Step 4: Compare up to three studio offers.** In the **Multi-Offer Matrix**, enter each competing studio's rent, commission and deductions to see how they rank on your own numbers.

**Step 5: Run the realistic simulation.** Set working days per month, appointments per day, average ticket and no-show rate. The tool derives three months from those inputs: **Conservative / Slow** (higher cancellations, +10%, and lower volume, -15%), **Realistic / Expected** (your scheduled days with your no-show rate), and **Peak / Busy** (near-zero cancellations and higher demand, +20%).

**Step 6: Project the annual cash flow.** Project 52 weeks with seasonal demand and time off. Booth rent usually stays payable while you are away and commission does not, so this view shows how holidays, conventions and quiet months move each model's annual total.

**Step 7: Model the studio floor plan.** In the owner's view, enter the station roster, fixed commercial overhead (lease, rates, insurance, staff) and occupancy, then see how many stations must be filled for the studio to cover its overhead under each model.

**Step 8: Assign consumables and supplies.** For each line item, including supplies, laundry and sterilisation, card fees and utilities, assign it to the artist or the studio under each model. Price an item **monthly**, **per procedure** or as a **percentage of gross** (for card fees). A standard piercing kit (needles, gloves and barriers, skin prep, sterilisation indicators) is included and editable, and you can add your own line items with the custom consumables builder.

**Step 9: Read the break-even curve.** The chart plots net earnings against monthly revenue with four lines: the artist and the owner, each under booth rent and under commission. Each line has its own pattern (solid, dashed, dotted, dash-dot) so it still reads when printed in black and white. Where the lines cross is the break-even revenue from Step 1.

**Step 10: Print, save, share or export.** Print the one-page A4 / US Letter summary, save a deal as a **Saved Preset** in your own browser under a name such as "City Centre Offer", copy a **Share link** that opens this exact deal for anyone you send it to, or download the full calculation as **CSV or JSON**.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1: Flat commission vs fixed weekly rent.**
An artist enters a booth rent of £150 per week, a studio commission of 40%, and 48 working weeks per year. The comparison shows what the artist keeps and what the studio receives under each model on the same revenue, and the **Break-Even Monthly Revenue** marks the point where the two cost the same. Below that monthly figure, the 40% commission costs the artist less; above it, the £150 weekly rent does.

**Example 2: Tiered commission with a minimum guarantee.**
A studio offers a sliding scale: a lower cut on the first revenue bracket and a higher cut above it. The artist switches on **Tiered / Sliding-Scale Commission**, enters up to three monthly revenue brackets, and reads the **blended effective rate**. They then switch on **Minimum Guarantee** to check the studio's take under its commission versus a minimum weekly floor, whichever is higher, to see how the guarantee changes the artist's take-home in a slow month.

**Example 3: Hybrid rent-plus-commission with a walk-in split.**
An artist takes a deal with a lower base rent plus a reduced commission cut, and uses the **Walk-In vs Custom Client Split** to separate shop-provided foot traffic from their own appointments. They run the **Realistic Simulation** with their working days per month, appointments per day, average ticket and no-show rate to see the conservative, realistic and peak months, then check the **Annual Cash Flow** to see how holidays and conventions move the annual total under this hybrid model.
